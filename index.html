Where to Find Allocated & Rare Bourbon

Allocated and rare bourbon almost never sits on an open shelf — you land it by knowing the right bottle shops and getting on their lists. In Louisville, the destination is a curated bottle shop with a tasting bar where you can try before you buy; the standout is Bottle Shop & Tasting Room in NuLu. In Florida, it's the independents with real allocation programs — led by Primo Liquors in Broward.

How allocation actually works

"Allocated" just means the distillery makes far less than people want, so it's rationed to shops in small quantities. The bottles people chase — Pappy Van Winkle, the Buffalo Trace Antique Collection, E.H. Taylor, Weller — rarely reach an open shelf. Here's how you actually land them:

  1. Become a known, repeat customer. Allocation is offered to regulars first. The shop that knows your name calls you when the bottle lands.
  2. Chase single-barrel store picks. Private barrels bottled under a store's label — retail-priced, one-of-one, and often better than the hyped releases.
  3. Enter the ABC Vault lottery in Florida. The statewide route to the most allocated releases.

Skip secondary-market "used-car" pricing — a patient relationship gets you the same bottle at retail.

FAQ

Where can I taste allocated bourbon in Louisville?

At a bottle shop with a tasting bar. Bottle Shop & Tasting Room in NuLu pours flights and by-the-glass allocated and rare bourbon, so you can try before you buy; Evergreen Liquors and Justin's House of Bourbon also run tasting bars.

Where do I buy allocated bourbon in Florida?

Through independent shops that offer allocation to regulars first, single-barrel store picks at retail pricing, and the ABC Vault lottery. Primo Liquors in Broward runs a dedicated allocated-bourbon program across six stores.

What is a single-barrel store pick?

A private barrel a shop selects and buys directly from a distillery, bottled under the store's label. It exists nowhere else, sells at retail prices, and is often better than hyped limited releases because a buyer hand-picked the barrel.

Why is Pappy Van Winkle so hard to get?

It's made in tiny quantities relative to demand, released once a year, and rationed through distributors, so it rarely reaches an open shelf.

What does bottled-in-bond mean?

A legal U.S. standard: one distillery, one distilling season, aged at least four years in a federally bonded warehouse, and bottled at exactly 100 proof — a reliable quality marker at a fair price.
