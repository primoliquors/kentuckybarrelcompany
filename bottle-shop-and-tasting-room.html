Bottle Shop and Tasting Room, Louisville

The bottle shop and tasting room model, and why it is the most practical way to buy allocated bourbon in Louisville.

A bottle shop and tasting room is a retail liquor store with a licensed tasting bar attached. You order a pour of something by the glass, decide whether you actually like it, and buy the full bottle off the retail shelf on the same visit. For allocated and rare bourbon, that sequence matters more than it does for anything else on the shelf.

Why tasting first changes the math

Allocated bourbon rarely sits on an open shelf, and most of it gets bought blind on reputation. A bottle you have never tasted can run several hundred dollars, and barrels vary. A tasting bar lets you spend the price of a pour instead of the price of a bottle to find out whether a given barrel suits you.

That is the whole argument for the format. Louisville built the bottle-shop-and-tasting-bar model around it, and Bottle Shop and Tasting Room in NuLu is the name locals point to first.

How to use a tasting room when you are hunting a specific bottle

Ask what is open by the glass before you ask what is for sale. Open bottles tell you what the shop actually has depth in, and a shop that pours a rare bourbon by the glass is usually a shop that sees it more than once.

Ask about store picks. Single-barrel store selections are the one category where a shop's own palate is the product, and tasting one before buying is the entire point of the format.

Ask how the allocation list works. Most allocated bottles never reach the shelf, and getting on a list early is worth more than showing up on release day.
