This Week in Drinks

Industry news, curated — with our take on what it actually means for people buying bottles. Updated every Monday.

Week of August 24, 2026

Bardstown Bourbon Company's two fall releases hit shelves nationwide this week — both real quantities, both $140

Bardstown released 2026 Discovery, a blend of 7-, 10-, and 11-year bourbons finished four months in Garryana oak at 66.4% abv, alongside Lochs of Jura Barrel Finish, a bourbon-and-rye blend finished 32 months in ex-Sherry casks at 52% abv. Both went nationwide August 21 at $139.99, in limited but real distribution rather than lottery-only quantities. At that price you're paying for the barrel-finishing work, not scarcity alone — worth comparing side by side if your shop gets both in, since they're built from similar base stock but pushed in very different directions. via Shanken News Daily

Maker's Mark's oldest bourbon yet is landing September 1 — and it's actually allocated, not just marketed that way

The fourth annual Cellar Aged release blends 11-, 12-, and 14-year-old Maker's Mark at cask strength — 112.1 proof — making it the most mature mix the distillery has put into this series so far. It reaches select U.S. retailers September 1 at $175 before expanding internationally later in the year. Maker's doesn't usually release anything this old, so if your shop has a track record of getting allocated Maker's drops, get on the list now instead of waiting to see it on a shelf. via Fred Minnick

Redemption's relaunch is working — now it's adding a $50 bonded bourbon and bringing back its 18-year

Deutsch Family's revamped Redemption brand has grown to roughly 85,000 cases with high-single-digit growth this year, in a bourbon segment that's mostly shrinking. It's following that up with Single Barrel Bonded Bourbon — six years old, 100 proof, $49.99 — going nationwide in September, plus a return of the Ancients 18-Year at $400 in limited fall quantities. Redemption doesn't have MGP mystique or allocation hype behind it; it's succeeding on a straightforward bottled-in-bond value proposition, which makes the $50 release worth a look if you're tired of paying up for less transparent sourcing. via Shanken News Daily

Diageo's layoffs reach the whiskey side too — Bulleit and George Dickel sit under the same cost-cutting umbrella

Diageo's roughly 2,000 job cuts and $1 billion cost-reduction plan cover the whole company, and its American whiskey brands — Bulleit, George Dickel, I.W. Harper — aren't carved out. Deep cuts to marketing and support staff don't change what's already aging in a rickhouse, but they can affect how hard a brand gets pushed into new markets or how much gets spent defending shelf space against smaller, faster-growing craft labels. If Bulleit is a staple on your list, it isn't going anywhere soon, but it's worth watching over the next year rather than assuming business as usual. via VinePair

Week of August 17, 2026

Heaven Hill's new Double Mash is the bottled-in-bond release actually worth buying this month

Heaven Hill's new Bottled-in-Bond Double Mash blends two-thirds of its standard bourbon mash bill with one-third of its wheated recipe — the distillery says it's the first time it's combined both mashbills into a single bonded release. It's 9 years old, 100 proof, and $49.99, rolling out nationwide this month. An age-stated, bonded bourbon at a real-world price from one of Kentucky's largest producers, sold at normal volume instead of lottery quantities, is worth prioritizing over most of what else is on shelves right now. via Forbes

Chattanooga Whiskey finished a bourbon blend in Moldovan ice wine casks — distillery-only, $59.99

The eighth release in Chattanooga's Barrel Finishing Series blends five malt whiskeys and one bourbon, finished in casks from one of Moldova's most decorated wineries. It went on sale August 13 at the brand's Experimental Distillery in Chattanooga for $59.99, with no wider release announced yet. If you're within driving distance, this is a straightforward walk-in buy; if not, it's one to watch for a possible broader rollout later, the way earlier entries in this series have gotten one. via The Whiskey Wash

Never Say Die is opening a Lexington brand home this fall — and it'll be the only place to get its next bourbon

The transatlantic bourbon brand — distilled and aged more than six years in Kentucky, then shipped to England for further maturation at White Peak Distillery — is opening its first brand home in downtown Lexington this fall, pairing a micro-distillery with an English-style pub. It's reportedly the only place anyone will be able to taste and bottle "Double Ocean," a bourbon that makes two transatlantic crossings instead of one. Worth a note on the calendar for a fall Kentucky trip; single-location exclusives like this tend to disappear fast. via Breaking Bourbon

Circle City's 50/50 Club blending program is expanding into Illinois and Wisconsin

Circle City Whiskey, an independent bottler that blends barrels from two different distilleries into a single custom release, is bringing its 50/50 Club program to retailers and clubs in Indiana, Wisconsin, and Illinois after growing more than 100% year-over-year. It's not a Kentucky operation, but the model is worth watching: taking barrels that didn't fit a brand's single-barrel program and rebottling them as a blend is a growing way independent bottlers stretch limited stock into something genuinely one-off. If your shop does barrel picks, it's worth asking whether they've got access to this program. via BevNET

Week of August 10, 2026

Stetson's first bourbon isn't a novelty bottle — it's a $70 Green River/Bardstown blend

The western-wear brand's debut release is a 7-year-old, 100-proof bourbon blending stock from Green River Distilling and Bardstown Bourbon Company — the first time those two producers have combined for a joint release. It's priced around $70-75, sold through Stetson's own store and site, and ships to 40 states. Celebrity-branded whiskey is usually a pass, but two real Kentucky distilleries and an actual age statement is a different proposition than a flavored cash-in; worth a taste before writing it off as merchandise. via Forbes

Frey Ranch is selling access, not whiskey — $999 a year for 500 members

The Nevada grain-to-glass distillery capped a new "Tractor Tailgate Club" at 500 memberships for $999 annually, which buys VIP access to limited releases like the Harvester Series and Five Grain Bourbon, virtual tastings with the distillers, and a discount at the tasting room — not necessarily the bottles themselves. It's worth watching as a model: paid membership tiers instead of lottery entries are one way distilleries are managing allocation demand. If a Kentucky producer runs the same playbook, this is roughly what it'll look like. via The Whiskey Wash

Chicken Cock Whiskey has a new owner — and it comes with two Kentucky tasting rooms

Next Century Spirits bought Chicken Cock Whiskey along with three other Grain & Barrel Spirits brands (Dixie Southern Vodka, Elvis Whiskey, and the E.G. Booz mark) plus the Circa 1856 tasting rooms in Bardstown and Louisville — NCS's first real footprint in Kentucky. Ownership changes are usually the moment sourcing or mash bill quietly shifts, so if Chicken Cock is a regular pour for you, it's worth comparing future bottlings against what you're used to rather than assuming continuity. via Shanken News Daily

The data confirms it: bourbon over $25 is the only part of the category still growing

Impact Databank figures show the $25-and-up bourbon segment grew 3.5% in volume last year to just under 11 million cases even as the overall category shrank 1%, with Maker's Mark leading the tier at 2.25 million cases. Distillers have every incentive to keep pushing new releases and shelf space toward that price band rather than the entry level. If your budget has crept past $25 a bottle without you deciding that on purpose, this is why. via Shanken News Daily

Week of August 3, 2026

RNDC is winding down — and your local shelf will feel it

Republic National, one of the country's biggest spirits distributors, filed Chapter 11 last week to wind down what's left of its business after selling most of its markets off to Reyes, Breakthru, Martignetti and others. For hunters, the story isn't the bankruptcy — it's the reshuffle. When a state's distribution changes hands, allocation math gets redone from scratch: accounts get re-ranked, drop schedules slip, and stores that never saw certain bottles suddenly do (and vice versa). If your usual shop sits in a former RNDC market, the next few months are worth watching closely. via Shanken News Daily

Garrison Brothers Laguna Madre lands August 8 — with a first-ever cask-strength single barrel

The seventh annual Laguna Madre — four years in American oak, four more in French Limousin — comes in at 101 proof, $349.99, and just 3,000 bottles. A thousand go at the distillery event on August 8; the other 2,000 hit key accounts and Garrison's own e-commerce starting August 14. New this year: a 135.4-proof cask-strength single-barrel version at $409.99, distillery-only. If this is your annual chase, the online window opens in days, not weeks — set the reminder now. via Breaking Bourbon

Old Forester's 117 Series goes Triple Char — and Kentucky keeps this one home

The latest 117 Series is the first Old Forester made entirely from whiskey distilled at the revived Main Street distillery in Louisville — a genuine milestone bottling for the brand. Triple-char barrels, 115 proof, 375 mL, $64.99, sold at the distillery and select Kentucky retailers only. That combination — milestone provenance, small format, in-state release — is exactly the profile that disappears fast. If you're anywhere near Louisville, go this week, not next. via Bourbon Lens

Old Elk drops three 10-year whiskeys at $69.99 — age-stated value with no lottery

A 10-year straight bourbon, a 10-year straight wheat whiskey, and a 10-year wheated bourbon, all around 103–105 proof, all $69.99, all nationwide. Double-digit age statements at this price barely exist anymore, and these you can actually walk in and buy. The 96% wheat whiskey is the sleeper of the three — a true aged wheat whiskey is a rarity from anyone. via Bourbon Lens

Cigar Blend turns ten — and it's Nancy Fraley's last one

Jos. A. Magnus marked ten years of Cigar Blend with an anniversary bottling that blends in 11- and 18-year whiskeys, finished in Armagnac casks — and it's billed as the final Cigar Blend from Nancy Fraley, the blender who created the category-defining original in 2016. Cigar Blend already trades hard on the secondary; a final-blender anniversary release is the kind of provenance collectors move on immediately. No proof or price published yet, so watch your shop's allocation list rather than waiting for a shelf sighting. via Bourbon Lens

Bulleit's first new core bottle in 15 years is a $29.99 bourbon-rye blend

Bulleit '87 blends the house Kentucky bourbon with its Indiana rye, finishes it on toasted American and French oak staves, and lands at 90 proof for $29.99 this September as a permanent addition — the brand's first new core expression in over 15 years. Nobody's chasing this one, and that's the point: a big brand putting a stave-finished blend on the everyday shelf at under $30 says something about where the mainstream market is headed. Try it before the ad campaign tells you to. via Bourbon Lens
